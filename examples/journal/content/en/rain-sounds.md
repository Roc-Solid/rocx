Everyone says they like the sound of rain, but rain doesn't have a sound. It borrows one from
whatever it lands on. Here is a small, unscientific catalogue, collected over one wet autumn.

## Outdoors

### On leaves

A soft, broad hiss with no single drops in it, like a radio between stations. It starts a few
seconds before you feel anything, because the canopy catches the first of it.

### On a tent

Each drop is a separate tap, loud and close, as if somebody were drumming their fingers right
above your face. Pleasant for the first hour; meditative by the third.

### On water

The quietest rain of all: a thin, high fizz over a pond or a river, and rings spreading where the
drops land. You hear it best from under a bridge.

## Indoors

### On a tin roof

The famous one. Heavy rain on corrugated metal fills a room so completely that conversation stops,
and nobody minds.

### On a skylight at night

A dry, glassy patter that seems louder in the dark. It comes in gusts, so you can hear the wind
without hearing the wind.

> Rain is the only weather you can enjoy with your eyes closed.

## A note on recordings

There are hours of rain recordings online, and many of them are lovely. They are also, nearly
always, the same rain: steady, medium, on leaves. The interesting rain is the kind that changes,
the shower that starts as a few ticks on the window, builds to a roar and then simply stops,
leaving the gutters talking to themselves for a while afterwards.
