# plate: Acme Plate

A food diary, in Roc Solid. You log what you eat to a day's four meals, and see the day's energy
against a target, with protein, carbohydrate and fat beside it. Foods come from three places: the
USDA's reference foods (about 7,500, seeded into Postgres), packets looked up by their barcode in
Open Food Facts, and foods you make yourself from a label. Recipes are made from foods and logged
like one. Your target is worked out from a profile, and the trends page charts the days and your
weight.

It is meant to be used, on a phone above all: the sections are tabs along the bottom on a narrow
screen, every control is big enough for a thumb, and everything works without JavaScript too.

## A tour

1. **`/`**, today: a ring of the energy eaten against the target ("860 kcal left", red once it is
   past it), a bar for each macronutrient, and the four meals, each with what was eaten (its
   amount, its energy) and a button that takes it off. The arrows go to the day before and after
   (`/days/2026-11-21`).
2. **Add to Lunch** (`/add?day=…&meal=lunch`): a search of every food, which follows the typing.
   Words match from their start, in any order ("chick brea" finds chicken breast); foods you've
   logged come first, then your recipes and foods, then the plainest names. Before a search, the
   foods logged lately. The meal can be switched with the chips at the top.
3. **A food** (`/foods/:id`): how much (a count of one of its portions ("1 cup, chopped") or
   grams), which meal, which day; what that amount has, worked out as it is typed; its nutrition
   facts per 100 g and for the amount; and where its data came from, with a link to it there.
4. **Barcode** (`/barcode`): type the digits under a packet's lines. One the diary has is found at
   once; any other is asked of Open Food Facts once and kept as a food. One it doesn't know offers
   to make the food yourself, the barcode filled in.
5. **Make a food** (`/foods/new`): as its label says it, per 100 g or per serving, with fibre,
   sugars, saturated fat and sodium if the label gives them, its serving, and its barcode.
6. **Recipes** (`/recipes`): a recipe's ingredients (searched for and added on its page, in grams),
   how many servings they make, what a serving has, and a button that adds a serving to the diary.
   A recipe is a food too (source `recipe`), kept up to date as its ingredients change.
7. **Goals** (`/goals`): sex, year of birth, height, weight, how active you are, and your goal give
   the energy a day; a target of your own wins. The targets follow the form as it is changed. The
   time zone says when your day starts.
8. **Trends** (`/trends?days=30`): the energy of each day of the last week, month or three months
   (weeks, for three months) as bars, the average day against the targets, and your weight, with a
   form that logs it (the latest weight becomes the profile's).

## Where the foods come from

- **[USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/)**, public domain (CC0): SR Legacy (2018), the
  classic reference of about 7,800 foods, and Foundation Foods (2026-10), newer analyses of a few
  hundred. [`migrations/0002_usda_foods.sql`](migrations/0002_usda_foods.sql) has them, generated by
  [`data/usda.mjs`](data/usda.mjs) (its header says how to run it again): each food's name, group,
  what 100 g has (energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat; fibre, sugars, saturated fat and sodium where
  the data has them) and its portions. Baby foods and the American Indian/Alaska Native foods are
  left out, and a name is kept once. It is 1.5 MB of SQL (330 KB as git keeps it), and the server
  applies it in under half a second on its first start.
- **[Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/)**, under the
  [Open Database License](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/): packaged foods, by
  barcode, asked for one at a time (`GET /api/v2/product/<barcode>`, with a `User-Agent` that names
  the app). A food from it says so on its page, and links to the product there. Its limits (100
  product reads a minute) are never near: a barcode is asked for once, and kept.
- **You**: foods made from a label, and recipes.

## Build and run it

From the repository's root, with the toolchain (`just setup`) and a Postgres (`just db`, which
prints its URL):

```sh
docker exec rocx-postgres createdb -U rocx acme_plate   # a database of its own
bin/rocx build examples/plate --client examples/plate/build/client
.tools/roc-active build --target=wasm32 --opt=size --output=examples/plate/build/client/static/rocx/app.wasm examples/plate/build/client/main.roc
node server/build.mjs examples/plate/main.roc examples/plate/build/plate
examples/plate/build/plate --port 18190 --static examples/plate/build/client/static \
  --database-url postgres://rocx:rocx@127.0.0.1:55432/acme_plate
```

Then http://localhost:18190. The first start makes the tables and seeds the foods. Set your
profile on Goals (the time zone above all), then log your first meal.

- `PLATE_PASSWORD=…`: the diary asks for it (once a browser, for 90 days). Set it whenever the
  server can be reached from anywhere but your own machine.
- `OFF_URL=…`: where Open Food Facts is asked (the tests' stand-in); the real one otherwise.

trends and weights, and the password. In Chromium: a search that follows the typing, a food's
preview as its amount is typed, logging it, and an entry taken off at once.

## Product calls

- **One diary, no accounts.** It is yours: on your machine it is open; anywhere else, set a
  password. Several people would each run their own.
- **An entry keeps what it had.** Editing a food (or a recipe's ingredients) changes what you log
  next, never a past day.
- **Energy and three macronutrients are tracked.** Fibre, sugars, saturated fat and sodium are on
  a food's page when its data has them, but not added up: too many foods don't say.
- **What isn't known isn't 0.** A food whose data doesn't give a nutrient doesn't list it.
- **Grams under the hood.** An amount is a count of a portion or grams, and is kept as grams with
  the words it was said in ("2 × 1 medium").
- **Targets are an estimate.** Mifflin–St Jeor, times an activity factor, ±250 or 500 kcal for a
  goal, never under 1,200; 25% protein, 45% carbohydrate, 30% fat. The goals page says so, and a
  number of your own wins.
- **A barcode is typed, for now.** See below.

## What it asked of the platform

What the platform didn't do, or did in a way an app has to know about, and what the app does
instead. None needed a change to the platform.

1. **Db reads no `numeric`, and writes no decimal.** A `numeric` column must be cast to be read
   (`kcal::float8`, then a JSON number that `Dec` reads exactly), and a `Dec` goes to Postgres as
   text, cast back (`$1::numeric`): `Db.Value` is `Null`, `Text`, `Int` and `Bool`. Every query in
   `Store.roc` does one or the other. `Db` reading `numeric` as a `Dec`, and a `Dec(Dec)` value,
   would take both away.
2. **A JSON reader for keys a record can't name is in packages/mcp.** Open Food Facts names
   nutrients with dashes (`energy-kcal_100g`), so its answer is read with `mcp.JsonValue`, and a
   food diary imports the MCP package. `JsonValue` would be at home in a package of its own.
3. **Bulk imports can't be done by the app.** `Http.fetch!` reads a whole answer into memory (64 MiB
   by default), and nothing unpacks zip or gzip, so neither the USDA's downloads (zipped CSV) nor
   Open Food Facts' dump (gigabytes of gzipped JSON lines) can be fetched and imported by a job.
   The USDA's foods are a seed that a Node script generates instead, and Open Food Facts is asked
   one barcode at a time. A streamed body (a line or a chunk at a time) and a gzip reader would let
   a job keep a country's packets up to date.
4. **No camera.** A barcode is typed. Scanning one needs the camera and a decoder:
   `BarcodeDetector` is in Chromium and on Android, but not in Firefox, so it would be a JavaScript
   or WebAssembly decoder called through `Browser.call!`. This is the first thing the mobile
   platform would do better.
5. **No client-side navigation.** A search result, the arrows between days, and the meal chips
   load a page. The search itself doesn't, as it reads its endpoint again in place (D-040 is the
   design for the rest).
6. **No installing it, and nothing offline.** On a phone it is a web page: no icon on the home
   screen, and nothing to log a meal on a plane.
7. Roc, met on the way: `targets`, `has` and `with` are reserved, and can't name a field, a local
   or a definition; a local or a lambda's argument can't share a name with a definition of its
   module (`grams`, `words`, `whole`, `sum`, `food`, `value`, `name`); `Paths` has pages only, so a
   form posting to an endpoint of no page names it as `Endpoint.url_of(Api.post_sign_out({}))`.
